Six ballast compartments, 1 at the bow to 6 at the stern. Water counts in whole units, never below zero. All pumps start LAMINAR, the overboard tally starts at 0, and a run is five cycles, 1 to 5.

Compartments 1 to 6:
Volume: 10, 8, 12, 6, 9, 7
Inception mark: 7, 6, 9, 5, 7, 5
Desinence mark: 3, 2, 4, 2, 3, 2
Starting water: 3, 6, 6, 2, 2, 1
Blow limit, whole line: 38

Water taken on, by compartment:
Cycle 1: 0, 6, 3, 5, 3, 3
Cycle 2: 4, 2, 0, 1, 1, 7
Cycle 3: 4, 4, 3, 0, 0, 6
Cycle 4: 7, 6, 7, 1, 3, 0
Cycle 5: 0, 6, 6, 3, 3, 0

Each cycle is five steps, in order, each finished on all six before the next.

1. Take on water. Every compartment gets its full table amount, even past its volume.

2. Scuppers. One at a time, 1 through 6. Anything above volume runs aft into the next compartment, leaving this one at exactly its volume. Going bow to stern, that water arrives before the next is checked, so overflow can cascade down the line. Overflow from 6 goes overboard.

3. Pump check, on post-scupper water. LAMINAR with water at or above its inception mark goes CAVITATING. CAVITATING returns to LAMINAR only when water is at or below its desinence mark. In between, no change. States change nowhere else. Log each change as (cycle, position, before, after).

4. Pump. Figure all six amounts from current contents first, then move them together. LAMINAR pumps a quarter, rounded down, overboard. CAVITATING backflows half, rounded down, forward into the next compartment toward the bow; compartment 1's backflow goes overboard. Nobody enforces volume here: backflow can leave a compartment over volume until next cycle's scuppers, and after cycle 5 it stays over.

5. Emergency blow. Total all six. Only above 38, not at 38, every compartment keeps half, rounded down, the rest overboard. Note the cycle.

Everything overboard goes on the tally.

Report. At the end of cycle 5, hand in: the six end quantities, position order 1 to 6; the six end states, same order; every state change as (cycle, position, before, after); the cycles the emergency blow fired; the overboard tally; and the balance check, starting water plus all water taken on must equal end water plus the tally.

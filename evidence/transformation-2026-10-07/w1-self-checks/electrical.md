- Word count: first draft was 537 words (wc -w, which also counts list numbers and labels as words). Cut to 398 by putting the data in compact rows, dropping the bullet markers and tightening the prose. No rule or number was removed.
- Banned words: a substring grep for cell, level, mode, normal, elevated, period, intake, spill, release, purge, lost, abstract, metaphor, analog, comparison, represent, correspond and 'stands for' found nothing in any draft. It also catches longer words that contain them, such as 'model'. The overflow step is called 'shunts' and the leak step 'leak and feedback'.
- Direction of feedback was unclear. 'to the stage toward the source' could mean any stage upstream. Fixed to 'the stage just before it, toward the source' (n-1 only).
- Direction of overflow was unclear. 'the next stage down' did not say which end counts as down. Fixed to 'the next stage toward the load'.
- A pronoun in the shunt sentence was unclear. 'so it can cascade' could refer to the stage or the excess. Fixed to 'so excess can cascade'.
- Stage 1 feedback: 'stage 1's goes to ground' was too short to follow, so it now says 'stage 1's feedback goes to ground'.
- Real-behaviour risk, breaker latching: a real tripped breaker stays open and usually dumps everything. The text says the stage keeps half rounded down, the rest goes to ground, and the check runs 'Every cycle', so the reader does not assume it stays tripped.
- Real-behaviour risk, instant breakdown: a real breakdown shunt conducts the moment the rating is exceeded. The text says ratings are not enforced during charge input or during leak and feedback. Overcharge waits for the next cycle's shunts, and after cycle 5 none come.
- Real-behaviour risk, continuous comparator: a real Schmitt trigger switches whenever its input crosses a threshold. The text says states change only at step 3, read on post-shunt charge, at most once per cycle. Charge moved later by feedback or a breaker trip does not flip a state until the next check, which also covers 'trip leaves states alone'. I removed the separate 'States are untouched' line to stay under the cap, because the 'only here' rule already covers it.
- Real-behaviour risk, continuous leak and diode equalisation: a real leak decays continuously, and a real return diode conducts until voltages settle. The text gives fixed amounts per cycle: a quarter for CLEAR, half for ASSERTED, both rounded down. ASSERTED leaks nothing to ground ('instead'). All six amounts are worked out from present charges before anything moves, so feedback a stage receives does not change what it sends.
- Real-behaviour risk, charge as a whole number: real charge is continuous. The text says whole units and never negative, and every division says 'rounded down'. The breaker rule also says which part stays and which part goes to ground.
- Inclusive thresholds: the text says 'at or above upper' and 'at or below lower', and 'in between, it holds'. The breaker trips only 'strictly above 38', and 38 or below means nothing happens.
- Everything goes to the tally: shunt excess from stage 6, CLEAR leaks, stage 1 feedback and breaker removals all go to ground, and 'Everything sent to ground goes on the loss tally' collects them.
- Rule left implicit: D < U <= C is not stated as a rule, but every stage in the data tables shows it. I chose not to spend words on it. No other rule from the plain statement is missing.
- Report check: the paragraph lists end charges (source end first), end states, state changes as (cycle, position, before, after), breaker cycles, loss tally and the balance-check equation. It has no figures or results; the only digit is the cycle number 5. The explanation never calls the line a comparison or calls any behaviour odd.

Plain statement of the engineer's process, nothing added, nothing dropped: There are six cells in a line, numbered 1 to 6; cell 1 is upstream, cell 6 downstream. Each cell has a level L (a whole number, never negative), a fixed capacity C, an upper trigger U and a lower trigger D with D < U <= C, and a mode that is NORMAL or ELEVATED. All cells start NORMAL. There is one global limit G and a running tally LOST that starts at 0. The process runs exactly 5 periods; each period has five steps in a fixed order, and each step finishes for all cells before the next starts. Step 1 (intake): each cell's level rises by that period's intake for that cell from the table; capacity is not enforced, so a level may exceed capacity. Step 2 (spill-over): cells are handled one at a time in order 1 to 6; if a cell's level is above capacity, the excess (level minus capacity) is added to cell n+1 and the cell is set to exactly its capacity; excess from cell 6 is added to LOST; because of the ordering, excess arriving from cell n-1 is in place before cell n is handled, so overflow can cascade down the line within the same step. Step 3 (mode check): using the level after spill-over, a NORMAL cell with level >= U becomes ELEVATED; an ELEVATED cell with level <= D becomes NORMAL; otherwise the mode is unchanged (a cell between D and U keeps its mode); every change is recorded as (period, cell, old mode, new mode); a cell changes at most once per period. Step 4 (release): first compute every cell's release from its level at the start of the step: NORMAL gives floor(level/4), ELEVATED gives floor(level/2); all six are computed before any change, then applied at once: each cell's level drops by its own release; a NORMAL cell's release goes to LOST; an ELEVATED cell's release is added to cell n-1, and cell 1's release when ELEVATED goes to LOST. Capacity is not enforced; a cell may exceed capacity after receiving a transfer, which is corrected only at the next period's spill-over, and never if it happens in period 5. Step 5 (global reset): sum all six levels; if the sum is strictly greater than G, every level becomes floor(level/2), every removed amount is added to LOST, and the period is recorded as a reset; if the sum is G or less, nothing happens. Output after period 5: each cell's final level in order 1 to 6; each final mode in order 1 to 6; the full list of mode changes as (period, cell, old, new); the periods with a reset; final LOST; and the balance check: sum of starting levels plus sum of all intake equals sum of final levels plus LOST.

# Bank E: the author's own writing (Ed's Battery 3). Cases only, near verbatim from Jake's 2026-10-06 drafts. Key is in keys/bank-E.md; the author is the key authority. Pass bar: 0 false alarms.

1. A system's value tracks grace, not its starting capability. Value can drain while confidence holds steady, because the drain happens off-book. Confidence placed in a guarantee that was never actually issued is leverage, and it works until the margin call. At scale you can't read every meter by hand, so the architecture has to keep the books by construction: a ledger of what actually happened and an auditor with an independent reference. Otherwise you're not scaling capability, you're scaling hidden debt, and the bill arrives all at once.
(Source: balance-sheet-message-2026-10-06.md.)

2. I use V=IR as a labeled diagram for three quantities. V is authority: permission, scope, instruction. I is the flow of effects: tool calls, state changes, writes. R is the gate: verification, friction. An open circuit is safe but useless, so protection is tuned resistance, not maximum resistance. The equation gives me names for the quantities and a way to talk about their trade-off. I'm not claiming electrical law governs agents.
(Source: metaphor-use-case-catalog-2026-10-06.md, entry 1.)

3. The clutch engages and disengages the transmission, separating the engine from the drive. My routing works the same way: the assistant drafts a scoped task, a person approves it, and only then does work begin. Between phases the context is disconnected; a new phase re-engages with only its authorized context. I'm describing the approval workflow I built, using the clutch to name its phases, not claiming transmissions prove anything about agents.
(Source: metaphor-use-case-catalog-2026-10-06.md, entry 14; clutch-skill repository.)
